# title_and_message_writing v1

General craft for the words on the slide: titles, kickers, and support text.
Register and phrasing conventions observed in the reference are the
StyleGuide's property, never this skill's. Where this skill and the
reference's observed practice conflict, the reference wins.

## Principles

A title is an assertion with a subject and a verb, carrying the figure that
proves it. "Peers trade at a 2.4x discount to the target" passes; "Trading
comps" is a label and fails. The test: if the data changed and the title could
stay, it is a label.

One claim per slide. The title asserts exactly one thing, and the exhibit
proves that thing. A title with "and" in it usually describes two slides.

The kicker locates; the title argues. The kicker names where the slide sits in
the storyline - the section, the question being answered. It never competes
with the title's claim and never repeats its words.

Titles across a section read as an argument. Read in sequence, kickers and
titles alone should reconstruct the storyline: situation, complication,
resolution. Parallel construction across sibling slides - the same grammatical
shape, the same claim structure - makes the sequence read in one voice.

Precision is the register. Prefer the measured figure to the adjective: "grew
ahead of every listed peer" beats "grew impressively". Hedge in the support
text if hedging is owed; the title states what the exhibit actually shows, no
more.

Support text earns its place. Body runs qualify, attribute, or extend the
claim. A bullet that restates the exhibit's numbers in prose is redundant in
both directions. Footnotes carry method and caveats; the source line carries
provenance; neither belongs in the title.

## Worked example: comps page title block

type.kicker reads "Valuation context"; type.title asserts "The target's listed
peers support a premium to the unaffected price", with the operative multiple
carried in a type.emphasis run inside the title. The support column's
type.body bullets each qualify one row of the exhibit - why one peer is
excluded from the median - and the least load-bearing qualifiers drop to
color.ink_muted.

## Worked example: risk page reframed

A label draft - type.title reading "Key risks" over four boxes - is rewritten:
the title asserts "Execution risk is concentrated in the first year
post-close", the exhibit ranks risks by exposure along one shared axis with
the dominant risk in color.accent1, and the type.body support states the
mitigation for that dominant risk. The claim dictated the exhibit's form:
a ranking, not an inventory.

## Checklist

- Does the title have a subject, a verb, and the proving figure?
- Would the title have to change if the data changed?
- One claim only, with no "and" joining two arguments?
- Do kickers and titles in sequence reconstruct the storyline?
- Does every support run add something the exhibit does not already show?

## Anti-patterns

- Label titles: "Overview", "Financial highlights", "Next steps".
- The kicker restating the title in different words.
- Adjectives where the figure should be.
- A title asserting what the exhibit cannot prove.
- Bullets narrating the exhibit's numbers back in prose.
